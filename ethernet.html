    • These are referred to as Twisted Pair cables
    • They come in two forms:
      • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP)
      • Shielded Twisted Pair (STP)



    • They both consist of four pairs of color-coded wires twisted around each other
    • The wires are twisted to prevent electromagnetic interference (crosstalk)
    • The main difference between both cables is that STP has a foil shield that covers the wires to further protect against crosstalk

      • UTP is mainly used in home networks
      • STP is mainly used in industrial networks


    Wiring Standards

    The 568B standard is the more commonly used standard nowadays



    • When both ends are wired using the same standard it is known as straight through (patch) cable
    • Straight through cable is used for connecting dissimilar devices. (e.g. PC - switch or PC - router)



    • When both ends are wired using the two separate standards it is known as crossover cable
    • Crossover cable is used to connect similar devices together. (e.g. PC – PC or switch - switch)
